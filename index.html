The program

Interview skill is practiced, not absorbed

Technical Interview 101 is built at the University of Florida and delivered on Edugator.app, a UF-affiliated, LeetCode-style platform made for structured practice. It is a guided path from fundamentals to interview-day readiness — taught live, practiced out loud, and backed by weekly problem sets rather than a pile of solutions to memorize.

  1. 01

    Taught live, not self-serve

    Every topic opens with real instruction — the concepts, the vocabulary, and the patterns that make the problems tractable.

  2. 02

    Practiced out loud

    Problems are worked through together, spoken aloud, the way you are expected to reason in a room with an interviewer.

  3. 03

    AI as a study partner

    You work alongside AI coding agents built into your prep — used to pressure-test your thinking, not to hand you answers.

  4. 04

    Two ways to take part

    A weekly in-person workshop with peer and instructor support for UF students, or a self-paced remote track with AI assistance.

A single session

What ninety minutes looks like

Every session follows the same shape, so you always know what you are walking into.

  1. First 20–30 min

    Core content, taught

    A focused lesson on the week's topic, from the ground up.

  2. Rest of the session

    3–4 worked problems

    Examples solved together, out loud, start to finish.

  3. During the week

    3 problems on your own

    Assigned on Edugator, due before the next session.

  • 5–6 hrs

    Expected weekly effort

    The session plus independent practice. Less than this and the series will not do much for you.

  • CS1 + Python

    Assumed background

    We assume Python basics and a completed CS1 course. No prior interview experience needed.

Fall 2026 7 sessions · no meeting Nov 11 or Nov 25 — UF holidays

  1. Session 1 Introduction + Arrays Oct 7, 2026 1 lesson · 8 problems

    Getting set up on Edugator and using an AI coding agent as a study partner rather than an answer key — then straight into the techniques that show up in more interview questions than any other data structure.

    • Series structure, expectations, and how to get the most out of it
    • Edugator setup, Python refresher, and Big-O notation
    • Two-pointer traversals and sliding window problems
    • Prefix sums, in-place modification, and space tradeoffs
  2. Session 2 Strings Oct 14, 2026 7 problems

    String manipulation, and the edge cases interviewers reliably probe for.

    • Palindromes and anagrams
    • Character counting and frequency maps
    • Parsing, tokenizing, and building strings efficiently
    • Immutability and the cost of naive concatenation
  3. Session 3 Hash Tables Oct 21, 2026 7 problems

    The single most useful tool for turning a brute-force answer into an optimal one.

    • Dictionaries and sets in Python
    • Trading space for time in lookups
    • Grouping and deduplication patterns
    • How hashing works, and when it degrades
  4. Session 4 Trees Oct 28, 2026 7 problems

    Recursive thinking, and the traversals every tree question is built on.

    • Binary trees and binary search trees
    • Depth-first traversals: preorder, inorder, postorder
    • Breadth-first and level-order traversal
    • Recursion, base cases, and depth vs. height
  5. Session 5 Priority Queues + Heaps Nov 4, 2026 7 problems

    Recognizing the "top K" shape of a problem, and reaching for the right structure.

    • Min-heaps, max-heaps, and Python's heapq
    • Top-K and K-th element problems
    • Merging sorted inputs
    • Scheduling and interval selection
  6. Session 6 Graphs Nov 18, 2026 7 problems

    Modeling a problem as a graph is often the entire difficulty — the traversal is the easy part.

    • Representations: adjacency lists and matrices
    • BFS and DFS on graphs and grids
    • Cycle detection and connected components
    • Topological sort and shortest paths
  7. Session 7 Common Interview Patterns Dec 2, 2026 6 problems

    Pulling it together: pattern recognition under time pressure, and the parts of the interview that aren't code.

    • Mapping an unfamiliar problem onto a known pattern
    • Talking through your approach out loud
    • Clarifying questions, edge cases, and testing
    • Full-length mock interview practice
