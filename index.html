Course schedule

Ten weeks, ten modules

One module per week, each pairing a short lesson with hands-on problems on Edugator. Expand any week to see what it covers.

  1. Week 1 Introduction Sep 16, 2026 1 lesson

    How the course works, how to get set up on Edugator, and how to use an AI coding agent as a study partner rather than an answer key.

    • Course structure, expectations, and the research study
    • Setting up your Edugator account and workspace
    • Working effectively alongside an AI coding agent
    • Python refresher and Big-O notation
  2. Week 2 Arrays Sep 23, 2026 8 problems

    The techniques that show up in more interview questions than any other data structure.

    • Two-pointer traversals
    • Sliding window problems
    • Prefix sums and running aggregates
    • In-place modification and space tradeoffs
  3. Week 3 Strings Sep 30, 2026 7 problems

    String manipulation, and the edge cases interviewers reliably probe for.

    • Palindromes and anagrams
    • Character counting and frequency maps
    • Parsing, tokenizing, and building strings efficiently
    • Immutability and the cost of naive concatenation
  4. Week 4 Hash Tables Oct 7, 2026 7 problems

    The single most useful tool for turning a brute-force answer into an optimal one.

    • Dictionaries and sets in Python
    • Trading space for time in lookups
    • Grouping and deduplication patterns
    • How hashing works, and when it degrades
  5. Week 5 Trees Oct 14, 2026 7 problems

    Recursive thinking, and the traversals every tree question is built on.

    • Binary trees and binary search trees
    • Depth-first traversals: preorder, inorder, postorder
    • Breadth-first and level-order traversal
    • Recursion, base cases, and depth vs. height
  6. Week 6 Priority Queues + Heaps Oct 21, 2026 7 problems

    Recognizing the "top K" shape of a problem, and reaching for the right structure.

    • Min-heaps, max-heaps, and Python's heapq
    • Top-K and K-th element problems
    • Merging sorted inputs
    • Scheduling and interval selection
  7. Week 7 Graphs Oct 28, 2026 7 problems

    Modeling a problem as a graph is often the entire difficulty — the traversal is the easy part.

    • Representations: adjacency lists and matrices
    • BFS and DFS on graphs and grids
    • Cycle detection and connected components
    • Topological sort and shortest paths
  8. Week 8 Dynamic Programming Nov 4, 2026 7 problems

    A repeatable method for the topic candidates fear most.

    • Overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure
    • Memoization vs. bottom-up tabulation
    • One-dimensional and two-dimensional DP
    • Knapsack and subsequence problems
  9. Week 9 Greedy Algorithms Nov 11, 2026 6 problems

    When the locally best choice is provably the globally best one — and when it very much is not.

    • The greedy-choice property and exchange arguments
    • Interval scheduling and merging
    • Greedy vs. dynamic programming
    • Constructing counterexamples under pressure
  10. Week 10 Common Interview Patterns Nov 18, 2026 6 problems

    Pulling it together: pattern recognition under time pressure, and the parts of the interview that aren't code.

    • Mapping an unfamiliar problem onto a known pattern
    • Talking through your approach out loud
    • Clarifying questions, edge cases, and testing
    • Full-length mock interview practice

About the project

Course, research, and platform — built together

Technical Interviews 101 is being developed at the University of Florida. Beyond teaching the material, the project studies how AI coding agents can support interview prep, using a validated pre/post survey to measure real learning outcomes.

The end goal: a complete course package, a research-backed assessment tool, and a clear answer to whether AI-assisted teaching actually improves interview readiness.
